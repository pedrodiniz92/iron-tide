Four ships, keys 1/2/3/4: 1 the BULWARK in play today, 2 LANTERN, 3 LANCER, 4 SENTRY. Every hull spans exactly 72 model units and draws the same 75.6 px on screen — measured from the geometry, not eyeballed (panel shows the numbers). G (or the panel button) runs the single-gun <-> two-gun animation, and each ship does it its own way: the Bulwark stows/rides out, LANTERN splits the centre gun apart, LANCER scissors the pair open around the nose, SENTRY slides the pair up out of the pontoons. The two-gun state is the real DOUBLE SHOT: guns exactly over the bullets at ±14 px (js/player.js), one gun on the centreline.

The width rule. The Bulwark's widest part is its tail box, a 72-unit box at x 0 (x −36..+36). Its booms stop at ±31.8, so the tail box sets the width. Each new ship has ONE part spanning exactly 72 units the same way and nothing reaching past ±36: LANTERN's stern plate, LANCER's wing spar, SENTRY's cross-deck bar. finishMob scales on the largest dimension and sync() adds 56/2.35×1.35, so equal raw spans give equal drawn widths — all four draw 75.6 px.

The guns. DOUBLE fires at ±14 px from the hitbox centre, and 14 px is 14/(75.6/72) = 13.33 model units, which is exactly GUN_X in js/ships3d.js. The tracers under the selected ship are drawn at those real game offsets, so you can see the guns sit on the bullet lines.

Same kit. These builders live in js/ships3d.js and go through the game's own bundle (surfMaterial / addHull / finishMob / palMat), so what you see here IS what the game draws — select one with index.html?ship=2|3|4.
